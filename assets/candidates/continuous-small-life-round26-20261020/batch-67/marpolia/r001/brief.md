# Frozen premodel design

Fossil tuft specimen concept for Marpolia spissa, interpreted as filamentous cyanobacterium, not a true plant. A compact preserved filament tuft on an original illustrative rock flake, not a complete rooted living macrophyte or exact ROM specimen replica. Nominal35mm tuft is illustrative, not specimen measurement. Attachment/free-floating habit unresolved; no attachment structure invented.

- Dense fine filament tuft, many strands branch mainly close to basal convergence
- No main stem, ribbon leaves, tiered whorls or roots; no M.aequalis central branching
- Shallow fossil-like relief on original small closed shale flake; grouped strand count artistic, no cellular resolution claimed

Retain concept A shale flake and shallow dark fan, reject its conspicuous thick tree-like upper branches. Model 52 very fine representative grouped strands starting in a diffuse basal zone, with paired splitting confined to basal quarter; upper strands continue slender and unbranched, slightly tangled asymmetrical fans. No single trunk, rooted holdfast or biologically invented attachment. Closed volumetric fine strands lie against shale, not painted texture. Entire tuft is a specimen concept; substrate underside is artistic. Actual ROM57841 photograph shows spreading tangled fine fans and no rigid central trunk; do not imitate M.aequalis historic retouched drawing.

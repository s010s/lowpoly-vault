# Immutable original premodel design

Conservative whole-organism reconstruction concept of Baragwanathia longifolia. Museum fossils and curator account support leafy axes, long narrow spirally attached leaves and a sprawling rather than high-tree habit. Total spread, branch connectivity, hidden surfaces, root architecture, pose and color are artistic; this is not an exact fossil individual or mature height estimate. Silurian/Devonian refers to documented occurrence, not a reconstructed ecosystem.

- Thick continuously tapering branched axes with leaves along their entire length, including low branches, rather than a bare trunk with a high crown
- Numerous narrow unbranched swept microphyll blades arranged successively around the axes in a spiral, producing bottlebrush silhouettes rather than broad fern fronds or whorled gears
- A low sprawling whole-plant habit with separated ascending and lateral branches, a modest artistic basal rooting connection, no flowers or invented terminal reproductive cones

Personally inspected three fossil photographs and the genuine concept. Preserve full-length leaf cover and branching silhouette. Correct the concept broad thick triangular leaves into slender swept blades approximately35-60mm long and3-5mm wide with subtle thickness; these are design dimensions, not measurements of the fossils. Reduce leaf count to roughly500-650 while retaining spiral attachment on all axes. Use a low horizontal-to-ascending basal axis with paired forks and unequal terminal branches, not a radial fan of disconnected shoots. Branch layout and small basal rooting connector are explicitly artistic where unpreserved. Keep upper branch spaces open and leaves single, unbranched and non-whorled. No soil base, large modern tree roots, cones or flowers.

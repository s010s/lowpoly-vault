Create an ORIGINAL low-poly 3D DESIGN CONCEPT sheet for FOUR small Cambrian marine arthropods, before any models are built. Friendly museum game props, matte faceted solid volumes, clean cream background, soft neutral studio light, no decorative bases, no text. Arrange FOUR ROWS, each with a large three-quarter dorsal view at left (front toward lower left, camera above right-front) and a compatible smaller top view and side view at right. Keep each animal fully separated in its own row. Views in each row must describe the SAME animal. This is stylized evidence-informed visual design, not a scientifically exact reconstruction. No face smiles, no cartoon eyeballs on eyeless taxa, no modern shrimp claws.
ROW ONE Emeraldella: muted turquoise/seafoam segmented arthropod with smooth broad semicircular head shield WITHOUT EYES. Eleven overlapping broad trunk plates tapering posteriorly, then two narrow cylindrical rings and a long thin needlelike tail spine. Two long flexible antennae sweep ahead. Slim ochre walking legs remain visible below flanks; understated pale swimming lobes. Body without antennae/tail 55 mm long, overall about 140 mm. Convex low shell, not a flat picture.
ROW TWO Burgessia: warm apricot and honey small eyeless arthropod, a single low broad semicircular thin domed dorsal carapace, NO eyes. Pair of long flexible forward antennae. Ten paired fine walking legs peeking beneath plus a tiny terminal pair. One exceptionally long slender unsegmented posterior spine about 1.4 times carapace length. Carapace length 14 mm. The shell should read as a quiet single shallow dome, not a segmented trilobite. Solid opaque artistic palette.
ROW THREE Tuzoia: plum-coral bivalved swimming arthropod, inflated two deep oval carapace valves joined along straight upper hinge. Strong outward horizontal ridge halfway down EACH valve, a few blunt marginal spines and pointed hinge ends. Tiny spherical dark eyes on short stalks protrude at front, paired short antennae, sparse understated ochre limbs and paired small tail fans beneath. Carapace length 90 mm. Shell should be deep sideways like a little purse, not a flat horseshoe crab; small polygonal plate marks subordinate to silhouette. Soft parts deliberately generalized.
ROW FOUR Sanctacaris: dusty blue shell with cream-peach underside. Broad angular head shield with two distinctive swept triangular lateral wings and two small lateral dark eyes. Six PAIRS of forward jointed grasping arms in a readable fan beneath head, with fine sensory branches; blunt safe tips. Eleven broad trunk segments taper to one broad paddle tail, paired swimming flaps and slender walking branches below. Body length 75 mm. A low broad swimmer distinct from the long-spined forms above. Hierarchy of broad quiet head planes, medium plates, fine limbs. Preserve actual thickness and negative space among appendages. Keep naturalistic relative anatomy and cohesive child-friendly low-poly construction.

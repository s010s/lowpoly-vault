## Tribrachidium
Original fossil-form specimen scope: low round form with three curved arms, not a confident living animal with invented appendages.
Primary2015 open paper https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4681345/ DOI10.1126/sciadv.1500800 describes actual fossils and interpretive CFD models separately. The view/positive versus negative relief orientation matters when recording arm chirality. Do not import its model, treat the fine 'tentacular fringe' as asserted living tentacles, or claim the new asset itself proves suspension feeding.

## Arête versus glacial horn
Official actual-photo reference https://www.nps.gov/articles/nunataksareteshorns.htm and https://home.nps.gov/glac/learn/nature/glacial-geology.htm . Arête is a long narrow serrated ridge separating two hollows, while horn is a compact pyramidal summit bounded by at least three steep glacial faces. Do not count a scaled or rotated shared mountain as two assets. Inspect actual Garden Wall and Flinsch/Reynolds examples, not just distant arbitrary mountains. Generic original geology, no exact site replication.

## Coal seam outcrop
Original gate: recessed coal band with cleat-fracture blocks bounded by more resistant host beds; not merely a black stripe.
USGS https://pubs.usgs.gov/circ/c1143/html/fig25.html shows actual coal face/butt cleats, with explicit AAPG copyright, consult-only. https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2006/1213/of2006-1213.pdf has actual cleated sample photos; https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/0277/report.pdf plateXVI B shows a whole Cooper seam outcrop. Preserve distinction between joint directions/terminations and perfectly regular decorative block grids. No mine-site, age or coal-gas measurement claim.

## Banded gneiss

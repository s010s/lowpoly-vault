## Verdicts

- **Waputikia r001 — ACCEPT BOUNDED.** The front/hero relief has a robust finite axis, irregular stout side branches that dichotomize into fine sprays, and a thin shale support. It reads as a one-sided fossil specimen rather than a rooted living whole; no root/holdfast or parent organism is asserted. Reverse/rear are correctly plain shale, but fine sprays are low-contrast at 512px. Source photos are low-resolution specimen thumbnails, not high-resolution replicas. The actual model is 0.102547705 × 0.007764795 × 0.095948383 m versus nominal 0.070 × 0.006 × 0.075 m; preserve that discrepancy in any later description. ROM reports specimen examples at 50 mm and max size 60 mm and says the affinity remains uncertain. This is not an exact museum replica or dimensional reconstruction. [ROM Waputikia page](https://burgess-shale.rom.on.ca/fossils/waputikia-ramosa/)
- **Linear dune r001 — ACCEPT BOUNDED.** Hero, top, and cross-section views show one very elongated, sinuous longitudinal crest with two exposed flanks and finite ends. It is not a rotated transverse ridge or U/star form. The cross-section is clear in front/rear/top; the long-axis side view is naturally small under common framing. It is a generic single-dune form, not a depiction of the field-level parallelism/spacing NPS describes, nor a named locality or wind reconstruction. [NPS aeolian article](https://home.nps.gov/articles/aeolian.htm)
- **Kettle basin r001 — REVISE (visual diagnosticity only).** The source geometry is a closed, irregular low glacial basin in continuous hummocky terrain, with a shallow pond; there is no raised volcanic rim or crater. However, in the final 512px hero and edge views, the basin slope is too subtle to reliably read as a depression without relying mainly on the blue pond patch. Revise the relief/framing/lighting so the closed depression is unmistakable while retaining a shallow natural kettle form and avoiding a circular elevated ring. The official NPS description supports a shallow pit left by buried detached ice and often ponded, so the factual concept is sound. [NPS kettles page](https://www.nps.gov/articles/kettles.htm)
- **Rimstone dam r001 — ACCEPT BOUNDED.** The final GLB presents one continuous, thin, irregularly sinuous calcite wall and one enclosed pool on attached cave floor; no stacked terraces, nested bowls, or repeated rings appear. The closed single-pool enclosure is an explicit study simplification, not a measured cave replica. Water is opaque/stylized; actual overflow and geological scale are not simulated. The official Mammoth Cave and Great Basin NPS descriptions support calcite-rich water pooling behind a dam and depositing calcite as it flows over the crest. [Mammoth Cave NPS](https://www.nps.gov/maca/learn/nature/stalactites-stalagmites-and-cave-formations.htm), [Great Basin NPS](https://www.nps.gov/grba/planyourvisit/cave-geology-in-depth.htm)

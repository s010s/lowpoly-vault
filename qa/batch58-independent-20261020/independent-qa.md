## Factual and design-source reconciliation

- The original four-panel concept is aesthetic input, not scientific authority. Calamophyton’s frozen brief explicitly rejected the concept’s thick, repeatedly divided modern-tree boughs; actual views show slender distal sprays and retained trunk bases. This matches the evidence-informed scope in Giesen & Berry’s 2013 primary study: attached digitate branches, abscission bases, trunk taper/broadening and a domed apex ([paper/repository](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/47293/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/669913)). Exact branch count, pose, hidden surfaces, color and illustrative height remain artistic.
- Perspicaris visual features agree with the original Briggs 1977 study’s bivalved-arthropod reconstruction scope ([Palaeontology Association record](https://palass.org/publications/palaeontology-journal/archive/20/3/article_pp595-621)); the museum’s feature-specific fossil page states the straight-hinged paired carapace, stalked eyes, antennae, ten flap-bearing thorax segments, seven abdomen segments and forked tail ([ROM / Parks Canada fossil description](https://burgess-shale.rom.on.ca/fossils/perspicaris-dictynna/)). The modeled counts are coherent, but soft parts, shell volume, color and exact specimen proportions are not settled by this stylized asset. Phylogenetic placement is debated and not claimed.
- The barchan’s crescent/horns and slope directions match the peer-reviewed morphology summary in Hersen et al. (2005), which describes a flat/gentle windward side, steeper lee slipface and two downwind horns ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1029/2004JF000185)); the NPS page independently documents its two forward arms under a dominant wind direction ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/grsa/learn/nature/dune-types.htm)). Its mesh is a generic teaching shape, not a field reconstruction.
- The current-ripple decision matches Taylor et al. (2024), which describes sand-rich current ripples as asymmetric, with gentle stoss and shorter steeper lee slopes ([Sedimentology article](https://doi.org/10.1111/sed.13177)); NPS likewise contrasts wave-symmetric with unidirectional-current asymmetric ripples ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/ancient-ripple-marks.htm)). No site, age or measured sediment claim is made.

# Prehistoric invertebrate asset research

Research date: 2026-10-16. Schema version: 2. Planning inventory only.

## Result and limits

Exactly **300 distinct genus-level planning records** are included. All have English and Chinese names, descriptions, scientific scope, distinctness notes and original low-poly modeling briefs. There are **124 direct**, **164 group-supported**, and **12 candidate** records. These are not 300 verified reconstructions: **90 are reference-ready**, while **210 still have a reference, form or taxonomy gate**. The 12 unresolved candidates also have priority `hold`.

The production gates are: 90 reference-ready, 162 needs-specific-reference, 34 needs-taxonomy-review, and 14 needs-form-review. A direct source can still leave anatomy or affinity unresolved; direct evidence is not automatic production approval. Group-supported briefs describe a proposed distinguishable shape that must be checked against a named specimen or primary figure before modeling. If that check fails, replace the slot rather than filling it with a recolor, size change or subtle synonym.

## Scope and diversity

The collection spans Ediacaran through Quaternary settings. Period tags are representative research anchors, not complete genus ranges or claims that all taxa coexisted. Epochs and uncertain alternative age assignments belong in the notes. The Megalodon Triassic tag is explicitly provisional and cannot be used as a confirmed genus range.

Practical subcategory counts:

- 99 arthropods
- 71 mollusks
- 35 echinoderms
- 22 cnidarians
- 21 early metazoans or uncertain early animal forms
- 17 sponges
- 12 brachiopods
- 7 worms
- 6 bryozoans
- 5 graptolites
- 4 other invertebrates
- 1 ctenophore

There are 207 living-reconstruction proposals and 93 fossil-specimen proposals. The latter are the sole representation for those taxa, not additional shell or body-part copies of a separate whole animal. Shell diversity includes uncoiled, knotted, cap-shaped, tubular, winged, horned, operculate and strongly unequal-valve constructions rather than relying on many near-identical ammonite discs. Flat or minute fossils may need an enlarged specimen presentation, but scale alone is never their distinctness claim.

No chordates, plants, algae, fungi, microbial sedimentary structures, traces, nests or deadwood are included. Graptolites are hemichordate invertebrates; their practical category does not imply they are chordates. Early problematic animal affinities are left explicit instead of being forced into a confident modern phylum.

## Evidence method

The 161 source records identify bounded evidence, institution, URL, access date, verification route and limitations. Of these, 106 were opened successfully and 55 were verified in indexed search results. The source types comprise 105 museum resources, 39 papers, 13 academic resources and 4 geological-survey resources. WoRMS is an academic taxonomic database and is used for name mapping only, not specimen morphology.

Royal Ontario Museum Burgess Shale taxon pages supply many named anatomy references. Paleontological Research Institution Digital Atlas collections supply group context and selected named fossil forms. Primary papers, university collections, museum studies and geological surveys supply additional distinctive forms and revisions. A broad virtual-collection landing page is not treated as proof of every proposed genus. Several publisher or PMC pages returned fetch errors or browser challenges; those entries retain `search-result` verification and a bounded limitation. 

Existing reference scans and reconstructions were used as anatomical references only. No models, meshes, geometry, images or artwork were downloaded, copied or created. The briefs are original planning directions. Source access or an open-access article does not grant permission to copy its figures, reconstruction artwork or geometry. Any later production must separately check image rights, taxonomic choice, anatomical interpretation and actual asset QA. No measured polygon counts, meter dimensions, licenses, published downloads or released-asset status are asserted here.

## Reviewed replacements and stable IDs

These five changes occurred after the initial 300-record draft; the final count remains 300:

1. `pohlsepia` → `paleocadmus`. The 2026 reinterpretation identifies the famous supposed octopus as a decayed nautiloid, probably Paleocadmus. Exact soft-body reconstruction and association remain on form review. Pohlsepia is retained only as a historical attribution alias, not a second animal.
2. `alienoptera` → `alienopterus`. Alienoptera is an order; Alienopterus is the intended genus. The order remains in taxonomic context.
3. `orthoceras` → `yochelcionella`. Replaces a weak straight-cone contrast with a compressed cap shell bearing a projecting subapical tube. Baculites retains the straight compressed shell slot.
4. `hamites` → `capinatator`. Replaces a near-duplicate U-shaft shell with a chaetognath bearing a conspicuous grasping-spine head basket. Diplomoceras retains the repeated hairpin shell construction.
5. `lichenoides` → `sollasina`. Replaces a weak cup-and-brachiole contrast with a plated body surrounded by many long plated podia.

No further final ID changes were made. Each genus occurs once. The separate `megalodon-bivalve` ID is deliberately unrelated to the shark Otodus megalodon; this is not a duplicate shark or a declaration that the bivalve genus itself is invalid.

## Important scientific cautions

- **Nectocaris:** retain the flattened, finned body and paired anterior appendages, but do not label them squid tentacles or assume a jet-propulsion funnel. The 2025 nectocaridid study supports chaetognath affinity and supersedes an unqualified cephalopod interpretation on an older museum page. Affinity and mouth reconstruction remain gated.
- **Scyphocrinites:** the distal chambered lobolith is a recognizable structure; a gas-filled float is not established. A later functional study argues for a modified holdfast. Do not force an upside-down drifting pose into the brief.
- **Kerygmachela:** the 2018 specimens support one median tail spine, revising older paired-tail reconstructions.
- **Dibasterium:** the inner and outer limb branches insert separately. Do not model them all as forks from one walking leg.
- **Chimerarachne:** a tailed spider-related arachnid with spinnerets, not an asserted direct ancestor of living spiders. Particular web architecture and venom are not established.
- **Belosaepia:** only its distinctive pronged internal shell is proposed; no unsupported complete soft body or second whole-animal slot is counted.
- **Pneumodesmus:** historical age assignments conflict and the preserved material does not justify a confident complete millipede body. Its two period tags are alternatives requiring review, not a continuous range.
- **Megalodon bivalve:** select a named species and identified specimen before confirming genus, age or heart-shaped shell. Traditional Triassic assignments can belong to Neomegalodon or Triadomegalodon. The taxonomy hold must not be removed merely because a megalodontid shell resembles the brief.
- **Hystrivasum:** check its treatment as a genus or subgenus of Vasum against a named fossil species; no separate Vasum asset is counted.
- Ediacaran forms and several Cambrian problematica may have recognizable fossil outlines while lacking settled animal affinities, life orientation or complete anatomy. Their taxonomic gates remain visible.
- Chinese labels retain Latin names plus clear descriptors when an established Chinese common name is uncertain. These are readable asset labels, not claims of official translated nomenclature.

## Explicit candidate backlog

The following 12 records are on hold, even though they occupy planning slots:

- Pneumodesmus: reconcile age and the limits of whole-body inference from incomplete material.
- Palaeophonus: verify species-level specimen, appendage proportions and defensible body reconstruction.
- Hystrivasum: resolve generic/subgeneric usage and verify the specific fossil vase-shell form.
- Conocardium: verify rostroconch identity, aperture and hood construction against a primary specimen.
- Ribeiria: check shell outline and beak interpretation with an identified specimen or primary plate.
- Strobilepis: verify plate arrangement and whole-body limits rather than extrapolating a modern chiton.
- Megalodon bivalve: lock a named species, accepted genus and dated specimen; exclude blanket megalodontid assignment.
- Richthofenia: verify the selected cone-and-lid brachiopod specimen and internal lid position.
- Eleutherocrinus: the stalkless condition has a source, but the proposed asymmetric ambulacral construction needs a specific specimen.
- Palaeocoma: verify generic identity and a suitably complete brittle-star specimen.
- Conchopeltis: verify affinity and what the cap-shaped fossil actually preserves; do not invent a soft margin.
- Sphenothallus: verify tube edge construction, actual branching and organismal interpretation for the selected specimen.

The remaining 164 group-supported rows are a larger taxon-specific reference queue, not a hidden claim of completion. Their exact names and proposed forms are filterable in assets.json. Highest-value next work is a named specimen or primary figure for each, followed by a silhouette comparison within the relevant shell, trilobite, crinoid, coral or sponge family. Taxonomic difference alone must never justify retaining an indistinguishable low-poly slot.

## Paired-form review guardrails

Four source-supported Cambrian pairs were independently flagged for low-poly shape overlap. Their evidence remains direct, but the weaker member now has a separate `needs-form-review` gate:

- Alalcomenaeus versus Leanchoilia: verify the fringed spatulate tail and flank outline against the pointed tail and filamentous great appendages.
- Plenocaris versus Perspicaris: verify carapace/exposed-abdomen construction and terminal anatomy; body length or scale alone cannot justify both slots.
- Louisella versus Ottoia: require specimen-backed papilla-bearing trunk organization to remain readable; a longer or thinner tube is insufficient.
- Burgessochaeta versus Canadia: compare discrete angled setal fans with broad overlapping dorsal bristle coverage; resizing the same worm is insufficient.

These are unresolved production comparisons, not declarations that taxonomy alone proves distinctness. Each weaker row contains bilingual guardrails and must be replaced if its supported geometry cannot remain recognizable at the intended scale. The stronger reference-ready row does not authorize producing its paired counterpart.

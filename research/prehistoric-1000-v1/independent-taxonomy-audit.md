# Independent taxonomy and distinctness audit

Audit date: 2026-10-16. Research-only inventory; no model was made, rendered, measured, exported, or inspected by this audit. This is an independent challenge of the proposals, not scientific certification of all 1,000 assets.

## Scope and meaning of the results

Initial full arrays examined: 400 vertebrates, 300 invertebrates, 190 environment records, and the ten immutable existing records. Flora was still being assembled during the first pass; the final 100-record flora supplement and revision reconciliation now follow below. Initial input hashes/counts are in `input-snapshot.json`; a final working-input snapshot was recorded separately in `final-input-snapshot.json` (working research only; delivered-file checksums supersede it for the delivered bundle). Authors may subsequently revise their arrays. This report does not edit their inventory files or own the consolidated count.

- **ACCEPT concept** means the written brief contains a potentially meaningful geometric distinction. It does not approve the anatomy, taxonomic assignment, period, source completeness, or a future mesh.
- **HOLD form** means the brief does not yet demonstrate sufficient visible distinction. Provide a named specimen and explicit side-by-side geometry criteria; if that cannot be done without inventing anatomy, replace one slot.
- **HOLD taxonomy** means an identity or synonym question requires a declared taxonomic treatment. Merely assigning a production gate does not prove two countable assets.
- **REPLACE/REWRITE** identifies a definite naming or evidentiary problem in the current proposal. A replacement must itself pass genus, alias, and silhouette checks.
- The pair matrix is an independent screen of the supplied briefs. Entries marked “brief” are design-risk judgments, not newly researched scientific conclusions. Primary/museum sources were independently searched for the consequential uncertain taxonomic claims, with links in the source register.

## Final disposition

The saved revision resolves the principal concrete naming, obsolete-reconstruction, shifted-species and log-padding defects identified here. The audit records **150 numbered pair/cohort checks**, comparisons to the **ten immutable existing concepts**, and **40 independently checked source references**. The final arithmetic is **990 planned additions + 10 existing records = 1,000**, with no duplicate IDs or repeated first-genus tokens in the checked arrays. This is a conditional planning inventory, not a scientific or mesh-distinctness all-clear.

Remaining priority: preserve the stated taxonomy/reference/form gates, especially near-identical long-necked marine reptiles, closely proportioned dinosaurs, similar invertebrate body plans, and simple leaf/frond forms. If named-specimen comparison cannot establish a visible geometric distinction, replace the weaker candidate rather than count a renamed, scaled or damaged version.

## Findings in the initial snapshot (see revision reconciliation for fixes)

1. **Alienoptera is an order, not the genus described by its row.** Rewrite the slot as **Alienopterus brachyelytrus**, using the original paper, or substitute another individually supported organism. The short forewings/functional hindwings are explicitly supported by the 2016 description. This correction preserves one slot; it does not create a second asset. [S01]
2. **Pohlsepia's proposed living coleoid form is superseded.** April 2026 research identifies decomposed nautiloid remains rather than an early octopus. Remove the old rounded-body/fins/arm-crown brief. Prefer a different well-supported organism; alternatively retain one explicitly revised fossil-study slot with the new paper and accepted naming treatment. Do not keep old anatomy merely because priority is “hold.” [S02–S04]
3. **Miragaia/Dacentrurus is an active contradictory-literature case.** A study published online in 2024 proposed synonymy; Costa et al. (2025) presents new material supporting separation. Do not confidently assert that Miragaia is invalid. For a strict distinct-count target, replace one slot, or document the adopted treatment and a genuine visible distinction before counting both as resolved. [S05–S07]
4. **Shonisaurus/Shastasaurus needs species locks.** Their generic names are not automatically synonymous, but the placement of *sikanniensis* has moved between them. Anchor separate slots to *Shonisaurus popularis* and *Shastasaurus pacificus* with appropriate references; never reconstruct the same *sikanniensis* skeleton under both names. The incomplete material and contested life reconstructions still require form review. [S08–S10]
5. **Hamites/Diplomoceras is a strong mesh-level overlap risk.** Both current briefs describe ribbed shafts joined by U-bends. NHM explicitly illustrates paperclip-like Hamites. Taxonomic separation does not rescue an identical paperclip mesh. Replace Hamites unless a selected specimen provides a visibly different coil/shaft topology. [S11; brief]
6. **Orthoceras/Baculites is another strong low-poly overlap risk.** Current briefs are two slender tapering straight shells, with only a subtle cross-sectional distinction. Taxonomic and internal/sutural differences must actually be modeled at the intended viewing scale, or retain one straight-shell form and replace the other. Also require a specimen-level Orthoceras identification, not the commercial use of that name for any straight nautiloid. [brief; IV source registry remains only group-supported for Orthoceras]
7. **Elasmosaurus/Styxosaurus/Albertonectes needs more than “very long neck.”** Published work demonstrates real proportional variation, so these are not asserted synonyms. The present briefs do not provide a useful three-way visible discrimination test. Keep the strongest one; hold/rebrief the others with documented normalized neck/trunk/head/fin proportions or replace them. Vertebral count alone is invisible in a skinned low-poly animal. [S12–S13]
8. **Carcharodontosaurus fossil-study must identify the specimen.** The Egyptian material historically attributed to it was named Tameryraptor in 2025; the Moroccan neotype remains Carcharodontosaurus under that treatment. A generic museum profile is insufficient to choose the skull. Do not copy the Egyptian reconstruction into a Carcharodontosaurus slot. [S14]
9. **Oviraptor crest needs its own reference.** Do not borrow the familiar Citipati-like high-crested skull. The comparative AMNH paper explicitly distinguishes the holotypes. No Citipati row was present, so this is misidentification risk rather than an existing duplicate. [S15]
10. **Nectocaris remains a distinct visual candidate, but its wording needs an update.** The 2025 study supports stem-chaetognath affinity and interprets relevant head structures accordingly. Retain the morphology cautiously; avoid an unqualified squid-style tentacle/funnel reconstruction. [S16]
11. **Do not collapse Mammut/Mammuthus or Brontosaurus/Apatosaurus automatically.** Museum/primary evidence supports the former as separate groups and the latter as separately recognized genera in a major revision. Brachiosaurus/Giraffatitan likewise has published proportional differences. Those are valid taxonomic starting points, not licenses to count near-identical meshes. [S17–S19]
12. **Aliases are sparse.** The 700 full animal records had no repeated first-genus token, but this does not establish synonym freedom. Only seven rows had nonempty aliases. The one exact alias collision, Otodus’s common alias “Megalodon” versus the bivalve genus Megalodon, is a homonym/common-name collision and must be disambiguated by group, not merged.

## Existing-asset and cross-domain checks

The immutable existing ten were reviewed by their actual descriptions, not their filenames alone.

- **horsetail-cluster vs Calamites:** conditional acceptance only for a woody arborescent construction with trunk/nodal branch architecture, not enlarged versions of the seven existing herbaceous stems. **Schizoneura** is a specimen of broad fused sheaths; **Sphenophyllum** has wedge leaves and climbing architecture. None should become another generic horsetail clump.
- **lepidodendron vs Sigillaria:** vertical scar rows and crown architecture offer differences beyond the existing helical diamond-cushion, dichotomous crown. Scar patterns must be visible at target scale. **Paralycopodites** must emphasize lateral cone-bearing branch architecture rather than the existing high repeated dichotomy. No extra Lepidodendron leaf/root/cone slot was present in the examined flora.
- **fern-cluster vs fern specimens:** standalone pinnae/fronds can be legitimate specimen studies only when a named morphology and different use are explicit. Hausmannia's broad fused fan, Matonidium's palmate rays, and Dictyophyllum's divided radiating system provide clearer geometry than another generic pinnate ground fern. Psaronius has a root-mantled trunk, so it is not simply the existing cluster made taller.
- **cycad vs Cycadeoidea:** a fossil armored trunk with embedded reproductive sites can be distinct in use and structure; do not add the existing leafy cycad crown. **Wielandiella** must remain a forked shrub; **Bjuvia** an entire strap-leaf specimen. The existing cycad is a short armored rosette, not a blanket exclusion of all superficially cycad-like seed plants.
- **conifer-tree vs Voltzia/Araucaria/Metasequoia/Sciadopitys:** require genuinely specimen-scale reproductive/attachment/topological studies, not detached ordinary branches added solely to inflate the tally. A whole generic conifer and its unassigned branch/wood/cone are not automatically independent. Agathis's broad simple leaves offer a more useful whole-tree contrast than another needle spray canopy.
- **uprooted-log vs hollow-fallen-log:** strong overlap concern under the explicit no-damage-variant rule. The new rootless hollow log can be an independently useful tunnel prop, but that is not yet demonstrated by the one-sentence brief. Recommend replacing it unless a distinct ecological-structure purpose and non-derivative construction is documented. Removing roots and hollowing the existing trunk alone should not count.
- **natural-arch vs sea-cave/lava-tube-entrance:** conditionally separate enclosed rock cavities from a freestanding through-arch; the opening alone is not sufficient. Do not produce the same arch with different rock material.
- **stratified-cliff vs loess-bluff/cross-bedded-sandstone/folded-strata-outcrop/angular-unconformity-outcrop:** educational cross-sections can be distinct if internal bedding geometry, not new erosion damage or coloration, is the main object. A second generic layered wall fails.
- **three-toed-track vs pterosaur-manus-pes-trackway:** acceptable only if the paired hand/foot relationship and differently shaped impressions are unmistakable. Do not label three-pronged hand impressions as another generic three-toed dinosaur track. Sauropod manus/pes morphology is a clearer separate trackway form.
- **dinosaur-nest:** no second animal-specific nest/egg arrangement was found in the examined 190 environment entries. A future “oviraptor nest” with the same loose paired eggs would duplicate it.
- **coquina-outcrop vs named shell specimens:** bulk lithified shell-fragment rock has a separate geological construction/use; do not add living whole animal plus its own shell solely as two assets. No same-genus living/shell duplicate was found among the 300 invertebrates.
- **stromatopora vs stromatolite-mound/thrombolite-mound/oncoid-bed:** similar names do not imply synonymy. However, their geometry must distinguish canal-bearing sponge skeleton, laminated microbial mound, clotted microbial carbonate, and separately coated grains; four generic textured lumps would fail.
- **Megalodon bivalve vs Otodus alias “Megalodon”:** retain disambiguated group labels and scoped alias keys. This exact text collision is not biological duplication. It must not cause an automated dedup tool to delete the bivalve.
- **Archaeopteris vs Archaeopteryx:** similar strings, completely different entities; no fuzzy-string merge.

## Organ/fossil representation findings

The 400 vertebrate rows include 17 fossil-specimen plans and no duplicated named living+fossil genus. Their existence is not inherently padding. However, “fossil study” is not a general escape hatch for an indistinguishable second animal. Each must identify preserved material, missing parts, specimen utility and why this is a useful model rather than an arbitrary damaged alternate.

Especially inspect Carcharodontosaurus, Kaprosuchus, Titanichthys, Elpistostege, Gogonasus, Rhizodus, Kelenken and Icadyptes: the current source gate is appropriate, but the display name/description should make the planned specimen representation obvious. Flora morphogenera are different names for organs, not automatically different complete plants. The reviewed compact flora explicitly excluded several associated organs (for example Caytonia/Sagenopteris and Dicroidium/Pteruchus/Umkomasia), which is good. Preserve that rule in final assembly. A comprehensive organ-association audit requires the complete flora array and specific literature for any added morphogenus.

## Practical acceptance test before any production

1. Pick a species and named specimen/reconstruction reference; record historical combinations and disputed associations separately from confirmed aliases.
2. Draw or describe both closest neighbors at the same scale and pose. Name at least two evidence-supported geometry differences, preferably including one major silhouette/topological difference. This is a proposed QA rule, not a claim that such a test occurred.
3. Check that the difference survives low-poly simplification and intended viewing distance. Hidden sutures, microscopic spicules, unmodeled teeth and differing vertebral counts do not qualify.
4. For a fossil or organ exception, identify a distinct teaching/scene use and forbid a second counted representation of the same taxon unless explicitly justified under the contract.
5. If the comparison fails, replace the weaker candidate. Do not use alternative color, juvenile/adult, male/female, density, damage, pose or scaling as replacements.
6. Rerun ID/genus/alias checks after edits, including the existing generic concepts. Keep unresolved candidates clearly labeled; exact arithmetic is not evidence of scientific or visual distinctness.

### Flora source/alias decisions

- Confirmed or historical combinations (e.g. Rhynia major / Aglaophyton, the Wielandiella combinations, Iodes/Biceratocarpum) should be distinguishable from associated-organ names. The current aliases label associations as “not a synonym,” which is useful for humans but not safe to treat as taxonomic synonym assertions in software. Prefer a separate structured organ-association field if schema extension is possible; otherwise keep the qualification and do not strip it blindly.
- No separate Annularia, Calamostachys, Lagenostoma, Sagenopteris, Pteruchus, Umkomasia, Macginicarpa, or Platananthus counted row was found. These explicit exclusions help prevent organ-level padding.
- Nidula baltica's 2014 paper was corrected/validated by a later corrigendum, including erroneous figure captions. The organism remains a valid candidate; add the correction rather than deleting it. [S27–S28]
- Quatsinoporites is a distinct taxon, but direct reference availability does not imply its fragment has a sufficiently distinctive usable low-poly representation. Keep incomplete fossil material visibly limited; never infer a complete conk.
- Extant genera such as Marsilea, Salvinia, Azolla, Sciadopitys, Nelumbo and Halimeda require fossil-specific identity/range evidence. Their presence does not by itself mean the flora is modern; neither does their modern appearance prove prehistoric stasis. The current study mostly treats this correctly with specimen references and gates.

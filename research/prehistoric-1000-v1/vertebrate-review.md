# Prehistoric vertebrate planning inventory

Final research save: 2026-10-16. Schema version 2. This is a planning catalogue, not a model collection or a claim that 400 scientifically approved, visibly distinct meshes exist.

- 110 dinosaurs, 25 pterosaurs, 35 marine reptiles, 17 non-mammalian synapsids, 28 other reptiles, 25 amphibian-form tetrapods, 70 fishes/basal vertebrates, 65 mammals and 25 birds/stem avialans.
- 382 living-reconstruction proposals and 18 single fossil-specimen studies. A genus has only one representation.

- Production gates: 40 reference-ready, 157 needs-form-review, 198 needs-specific-reference and 5 needs-taxonomy-review.
- Priorities: 40 P2 and 360 hold. Confidence describes the planning concept, not exact reconstruction: 40 high, 158 medium, 202 low.

- All records remain planned, in the prehistoric world, with proposed research packs. There are no meshes, renders, measured polygons, dimensions, downloads or invented licenses.
- Twelve standard geological period IDs are used. Biomes are deliberately empty rather than assigning unsupported habitats.
- 32 display names use established Chinese names with the Latin genus retained. Other names use Latin plus a Chinese group descriptor. Historical dictionary taxonomy is not adopted merely to obtain a translation.

## What evidence labels mean

Direct means at least one checked source names the taxon or specimen and supports a relevant stated identity/form claim. It does not mean every detail, age assignment, relationship, coloration, soft tissue, pose or original artistic instruction has been verified. A named comparison within a primary paper may be direct evidence; a directory homepage alone is not.

Group-supported records retain useful research proposals but have not received individual taxon-level verification. Their identity, interval and distinguishing shape must be checked before production. The 198 such rows are deliberately held. Their supplied forms are hypotheses for reference acquisition, not licenses to fabricate anatomy.

The five candidate gates are Compsognathus (ontogeny/integument and current interpretation), Scipionyx (juvenile fossil and current taxonomic interpretation), Seymouria (stem-amniote/reptiliomorph versus the practical amphibian browsing bucket), Clydagnathus (soft-body conodont/genus correspondence), and Volaticotherium (formation/age assignment). These gates describe the actual unresolved issue; they do not declare every genus invalid.

Reference-ready is a reference-acquisition status for a planning concept. It is not a passed mesh/anatomy/silhouette test. Forty concepts retain this status; the independent form screen moved six previously ready concepts back to form review: Tyrannosaurus, Allosaurus, Ankylosaurus, Sauropelta, Sinosauropteryx and Genyornis.

## Research approach and scope

Sources were actually searched or opened with web tools on 2026-10-16. The registry primarily uses museum, university, scientific journal and geological-institution pages. Museum species pages and specimen records were preferred over generic homepages. Indexed primary text was used where a publisher returned a timeout, 403 or browser-check page; this is marked search-result, with relevant access limitations. Access limitations are retained in the registry.

The initial dinosaur coverage was supplemented with individual fish, amphibian, mammal, bird and marine-reptile references. Examples include the Elpistostege complete skeleton paper, the 2024 Ptychodus body fossils, museum Xiphactinus and Diplocaulus specimens, Whatcheeria growth research, Beelzebufo anatomy, an articulated Spinolestes fossil, the revised Genyornis skull, and a Confuciusornis revision. Fossil representations of Elpistostege and Ptychodus are intentional specimen-use choices, not false claims that their complete forms are unknown.

Scientific facts and original low-poly briefs are separate fields. Low-poly masses, support slabs, restrained palettes and simplification rules are design proposals. The references provide no blanket permission to reproduce artwork, scans or models. Obtain rights independently before using images or geometry.

The prehistoric world spans many periods and places. This list does not assert that the organisms coexisted. Period facets are broad planning filters, not numerical dating or a complete genus range. Pelagornis is anchored to Oligocene P. sandersi and consequently uses paleogene, rather than the previous generic neogene assignment. A selected extinct species may anchor a genus also known from a wider interval.

Practical animal subcategories are not strict phylogenetic ranks: non-mammalian synapsids use synapsid; bird includes stem avialans; amphibian includes some early tetrapods outside crown Amphibia; fish includes basal vertebrates and fish-to-tetrapod transitional forms. These choices do not turn early chordates into crown fishes or synapsids into reptiles. No invertebrate, nest or track asset is included.

## Replacements and specimen locks

The only genus/ID replacement after the first 400-record draft is Miragaia to Gigantspinosaurus. Contradictory recent Miragaia/Dacentrurus treatments make an automatic synonym decision inappropriate. The replacement uses Gigantspinosaurus sichuanensis and the 2018 primary redescription: its large paired curved shoulder spines provide a clearer geometry route. The spine orientation must follow that material. No extra slot was created.

Consequential locks now written in the rows:

- Shonisaurus uses S. popularis from Nevada. Shastasaurus uses S. pacificus UCMP 9017 as a partial fossil study, with its absent rostrum visibly incomplete. Neither may borrow the disputed sikanniensis reconstruction; Shastasaurus may not borrow Guanlingsaurus liangae anatomy. Both remain form-held.
- Carcharodontosaurus is a skull study of Moroccan C. saharicus SGM-Din 1. The Egyptian Stromer specimen SNSB-BSPG 1922 X 46, reassigned to Tameryraptor in 2025, is excluded. Do not transfer its horn or historical reconstruction.
- Oviraptor is anchored to O. philoceratops AMNH/FR 6517. Its crushed type skull controls the head; a familiar Citipati crest is expressly forbidden.
- Tapejara is T. wellnhoferi; Tupandactylus is T. navigans GP/2E 9266. Historical Tapejara imperator/navigans combinations are alias warnings, not extra assets. The Tupandactylus crest apex is incomplete and fossil colors are not a living-color prescription.
- Brachiosaurus altithorax and Giraffatitan brancai use the 2009 comparative treatment. Longer/deeper trunk and tail architecture must survive normalized simplification. Neither may simply reuse the same African mount.
- Apatosaurus louisae CM 3018 and Brontosaurus parvus UW 15556 use the specimen-level diplodocid revision. Bony neck/shoulder differences are not yet proof of a visibly different skinned mesh. Both remain held for form review.
- Elasmosaurus platyurus ANSP 10081, Styxosaurus browni AMNH 5835 and Albertonectes vanderveldei TMP 2007.011.0001 have direct comparative references but remain form-held. The three-way normalized neck/trunk/head/fin test has not been performed. Vertebral count alone is invisible and cannot justify three assets.
- Arctodus is anchored to the referenced A. pristinus material, not unreferenced giant A. simus dimensions. Dromornis uses the referenced D. planei material without silently importing D. stirtoni dimensions.
- The Homotherium cub mummy supports preserved cub information; it does not authorize scaling a cub into an adult or asserting adult coat appearance. Castoroides does not receive a modern beaver paddle tail without evidence.

## Distinctness and remaining acceptance work

The inventory arithmetic is exact; scientific and geometric acceptance is unfinished. The independent audit identifies many close pairs that remain on hold. Some proposed contrasts may fail in an actual low-poly design. The correct response is to replace the weaker candidate, not exaggerate unsupported features or claim that different Latin names guarantee different assets.

Before production, select a species and named specimen or defensible published reconstruction. Compare nearest neighbors at the same pose and normalized scale. Require at least two supported geometry differences, preferably one major silhouette or topological difference, that remain readable at the intended viewing distance. Color, sex, age, pose, damage, density, uniform scaling and hidden microscopic characters do not qualify.

For each fossil exception, identify preserved material, conspicuous missing parts and a distinct educational/display use. Eighteen fossil studies are single representations, not an extra skull/skeleton/tooth variant beside a living version. Incompleteness alone is not a free distinction. Several still need a specific specimen before production.

The alias list is conservative and incomplete. It records known historical combinations where useful, including Dinohyus for Daeodon and relevant pterosaur names. No repeated genus token does not prove synonym freedom. Otodus's common-name alias Megalodon must remain group-scoped to avoid a false collision with the unrelated bivalve genus Megalodon.

## Validation at final save

The arrays were checked for exact counts, unique IDs/taxa/source IDs/URLs, valid source references, bilingual required fields, valid controlled values and planning-only status. No Miragaia row remains. Every unresolved gate has hold priority. No genus is counted as both a living reconstruction and a fossil specimen. No existing vertebrate-animal asset was supplied in the immutable ten, and no trace/nest/body-part variant has been added as a second representation.

The delivered files are locked for consolidation after this save. Later scientific resolution must retain an edit log and rerun count, alias and visible-form checks; it must not silently turn these proposals into released assets.
